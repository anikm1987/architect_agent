## Q1: PRA Updates on Operational Resilience and SS1/21 Since 2023

### What is established

**March 2025 hard deadline passed.** The transitional period under PRA SS1/21 / FCA PS21/3 expired on 31 March 2025. From that date, all in-scope firms — including UK Solvency II insurers such as Atlas — are required to demonstrate they can remain within their self-set impact tolerances for all Important Business Services (IBS) during severe but plausible disruptions. The regulator has confirmed this as a hard compliance point, not a further transitional milestone.

**PRA Dear CEO letter — January 2025 (Insurance Supervision Priorities 2025).** The PRA wrote to insurance CEOs in January 2025 setting out supervisory priorities for the year. On operational resilience, the letter:
- Confirmed the March 2025 deadline and called on boards and SMFs to "monitor and manage the risks arising from transformation journeys"
- Stated the PRA would review firms' implementation approaches ahead of the deadline and provide feedback
- Introduced enhanced post-deadline expectations: firms must continue to improve testing, with "deeper engagement with third party and outsourced providers to scenario test material operational disruption"
- Flagged legacy technology obsolescence and change management as ongoing risks

Source: [PRA Insurance Supervision 2025 Priorities letter](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/letter/2025/insurance-supervision-2025-priorities.pdf)

**CP17/24 — December 2024 (Operational Incident and Third-Party Reporting consultation).** On 13 December 2024, the PRA issued CP17/24 proposing amendments to the PRA Rulebook and a new supervisory statement on operational incident reporting and material third-party notification. Consultation closed 14 March 2025.

Source: [Sidley Austin — UK Operational Resilience Rules, January 2025](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/01/uk-operational-resilience-rules-are-you-ready-for-31-march-2025)

**PS7/26 — March 2026 (Final rules on operational incident and third-party reporting).** On 18 March 2026 the PRA published PS7/26, alongside:
- A new Supervisory Statement SS1/26 on operational incident reporting
- A material update to SS2/21 (outsourcing and third-party risk management), effective 18 March 2027
The final rules apply to UK Solvency II firms, including the Society of Lloyd's and managing agents.

Source: [PRA PS7/26 — Bank of England, March 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2026/march/operational-incident-and-third-party-reporting-policy-statement); [A&O Shearman FinReg blog](https://finreg.aoshearman.com/uk-regulators-final-policy-on-operational-incident-and-third-party-reporting)

**PRA 2026 Insurance Supervision Priorities (Dear CEO letter, January 2026).** The PRA issued its annual insurance priorities letter in January 2026. Operational resilience content:
- Post-March 2025 focus shifts from "implementation" to "demonstrable resilience" — boards must embed resilience into strategic decisions and major change programmes
- Continued emphasis on legacy technology risks, transformation management, and cloud adoption oversight
- Specific expectation for deeper scenario testing with third parties
- CBEST and STAR-FS engagement assessments continuing for higher-impact firms on cyber risk

Sources: [Mayer Brown — PRA 2026 Insurance Supervision Priorities](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/uk-regulator-sets-out-insurance-supervision-priorities-for-2026); [Clifford Chance Insurance Insights, January 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/insurance-insights/2026/01/the-pras-2026-insurance-supervision-priorities.html)

**PRA Business Plan 2026/27 (April 2026).** Published concurrent with this research. The PRA will:
- Continue operational and cyber resilience assessments (CBEST) in partnership with NCSC
- Review systemic firms' operational resilience self-assessments for cyber response/recovery capabilities
- Scrutinise novel outsourcing arrangements and concentration risks
- Assess how SS2/21 has been implemented across firms
- Continue the CTP regime with HM Treasury

Source: [PRA Business Plan 2026/27 — Bank of England, April 2026](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2026/april/pra-business-plan-2026-27)

### What is uncertain / contested

- The PRA has not published a formal post-implementation thematic review of SS1/21 compliance across the insurance sector as a published document (as of April 2026). Supervisory feedback has been delivered bilaterally or through the 2025/2026 Dear CEO letters rather than a dedicated industry-wide findings report. This may change given the Business Plan 2026/27 language on resilience self-assessment reviews.
- No formal amendment to SS1/21 itself has been published; the framework's core structure (IBS identification, impact tolerance setting, mapping, testing, self-assessment) is unchanged. Tightening has come through supervisory expectations raised in letters and through the new PS7/26 layer on top.

## Q2: Cloud Concentration Risk and Third-Party ICT Risk Guidance

### What is established

**SS2/21 (March 2021, updated March 2026).** SS2/21 remains the primary PRA framework governing outsourcing and third-party risk for PRA-regulated insurers. It applies explicitly to both outsourcing arrangements and non-outsourcing third-party arrangements (including SaaS hosted applications such as Guidewire Cloud). Key provisions:

- A SaaS platform delivering a core operational service will almost certainly meet the PRA's "material outsourcing" threshold: "weakness, or failure, of the services would cast serious doubt upon the firm's continuing satisfaction of the threshold conditions or compliance with the Fundamental Rules."
- Chapter 7 of SS2/21 specifically addresses cloud usage, setting out conditions for safe and resilient deployment.
- Firms bear ultimate responsibility for remaining within impact tolerances even where a third party supports delivery of an IBS — vendor BCP/DR plans are not sufficient in themselves.
- Exit plans must cover stressed exits, be documented, and be tested. Escrow or equivalent arrangements are referenced as a mechanism.

The **March 2026 update to SS2/21** was published as part of PS7/26 and becomes effective 18 March 2027. It strengthens notification and register obligations but does not fundamentally alter the materiality or cloud governance framework established in 2021.

Sources: [SS2/21 — Bank of England (original)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2021/march/outsourcing-and-third-party-risk-management-ss); [SS2/21 March 2026 update](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/supervisory-statement/2026/ss221-march-2026-update); [Escrow London — SS2/21 and SaaS applications](https://www.escrowlondon.com/news/pras-outsourcing-and-third-party-risk-management-pra-ss2-21-are-your-saas-applications-ready/)

**PRA 2025 Business Plan.** Stated the PRA intended to consult jointly with the FCA on ICT and cyber risk policy in H2 2025.

Source: [Pinsent Masons — Insurers' cyber resilience under PRA spotlight](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/insurers-cyber-resilience-under-pra-spotlight)

**ICT/Cyber Policy Consultation (H2 2025 — status uncertain).** The PRA and FCA signalled a joint consultation on ICT and cyber risk management policy in H2 2025. As of this research (April 2026), no final policy statement or completed consultation has been confirmed in available sources. This consultation may be in progress or may have slipped. This is an open research gap — the architecture team should check the PRA regulatory calendar directly.

**PS7/26 — Material Third-Party (MTP) Arrangements.** From 18 March 2027, firms must:
- Notify the FCA (via FCA Connect) before entering or materially changing any MTP arrangement
- Maintain an annual register of all MTP arrangements (submitted via FCA RegData)
- Cloud and data-centre services are explicitly cited as examples of MTP arrangements

Guidewire Cloud Suite will, on any reasonable reading, constitute an MTP arrangement for Atlas. This triggers pre-signature notification obligations, not merely post-implementation reporting.

Source: [Sidley — UK Operational Incident & Third-Party Reporting, April 2026](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/uk-operational-incident-and-third-party-reporting); [FCA PS26/2](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps26-2-operational-incident-third-party-reporting)

**Concentration risk (systemic angle).** The regulators' stated objective in PS7/26 is to identify "systemic third-party dependencies" and concentration risk through aggregated MTP register data. This is a forward-looking supervisory tool. It does not impose a direct obligation on an individual firm to avoid a particular vendor — but firms should document their concentration risk assessment as part of the IBS mapping for any cloud-hosted core system.

Source: [Sidley — UK Operational Incident & Third-Party Reporting, April 2026](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/uk-operational-incident-and-third-party-reporting)

### What is uncertain

- Whether the joint PRA/FCA ICT/cyber risk consultation launched in H2 2025 and what it contains. If finalised, it may introduce specific ICT risk management standards beyond what SS2/21 currently covers. The PRA Business Plan 2026/27 does not reference this consultation as completed.
- UK DORA equivalence. DORA (EU Digital Operational Resilience Act) is not directly applicable in the UK post-Brexit. UK regulators have stated their framework mirrors DORA's principles but there is no formal equivalence decision. For an insurer like Atlas with no EEA operations, this is informational context rather than a direct obligation.

## Q3: Operational Resilience Testing Expectations

### What is established

**Post-March 2025 testing expectations have been raised.** Following the implementation deadline, the PRA's supervisory posture has shifted from "are you compliant" to "prove you can stay within tolerance under realistic pressure." Both the 2025 and 2026 Dear CEO letters make clear:

- Scenario testing must now be "severe, plausible, and deliberately uncomfortable" — not theoretical tabletop exercises
- Firms must demonstrate actual decision-making under pressure, not framework documentation
- Deeper engagement with third-party and outsourced providers in scenario testing is explicitly required — the firm cannot test resilience in isolation from its critical service providers
- Resilience considerations must be embedded into major change programme governance, including when "introducing new products, technologies, or outsourcing arrangements"

**CBEST and STAR-FS.** The PRA continues CBEST (threat-led penetration testing for higher-impact firms) and STAR-FS (for non-systemic firms) as its primary cyber resilience assessment tools. Atlas's classification under enhanced supervision (following the PRA flag on the Q3 Solvency II near-miss) raises the probability that STAR-FS or equivalent engagement is in scope. Firms under enhanced supervision are expected to be more active participants in these programmes.

Source: [PRA Business Plan 2026/27](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2026/april/pra-business-plan-2026-27); [Mayer Brown — PRA 2026 Priorities](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/uk-regulator-sets-out-insurance-supervision-priorities-for-2026)

**Detection, response and recovery emphasis.** A notable shift in 2025/2026 PRA language: the standard is no longer "prevent attacks" but "detect, respond and recover critical services within impact tolerances." This has direct implications for how Atlas designs its target-state monitoring and incident management for the Guidewire Cloud environment.

Source: [LCP — PRA 2026 Priorities for General Insurers](https://www.lcp.com/en/insights/in-brief/pra-regulatory-priorities-for-general-insurers-2026-what-to-focus-on)

**Two-tier reporting model in PS7/26 (effective March 2027).** Standard firms submit a single operational incident report; enhanced firms must follow a three-phase model (initial within 24 hours, intermediate updates, final within 30–60 working days). Atlas's enhanced supervision status almost certainly places it in the enhanced tier, though the final classification criteria under PS7/26 will need to be confirmed.

Source: [Sidley — UK Operational Incident & Third-Party Reporting, April 2026](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/04/uk-operational-incident-and-third-party-reporting)

**Dynamic General Insurance Stress Test (DyGIST) — May 2026.** The PRA's next general insurance stress test is a dynamic (scenario-based, multi-week) exercise commencing May 2026. This is distinct from operational resilience testing but adds supervisory pressure on GI firms' risk management capabilities during the same period as Atlas's early programme phases.

Source: [Mayer Brown — PRA 2026 Insurance Priorities](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/uk-regulator-sets-out-insurance-supervision-priorities-for-2026)

### What is uncertain

- The precise criteria by which Atlas would be classified as "enhanced" vs. "standard" under the PS7/26 reporting tier are not yet published in operational detail. The firm should seek supervisory clarification.
- Whether the PRA has issued any specific written feedback to Atlas on its post-March 2025 operational resilience self-assessment is not known from public sources.

## Q4: Joint PRA/FCA/BoE Critical Third Party (CTP) Regime

### What is established

**PS16/24 / PS24/16 — November 2024 (Final CTP rules).** On 12 November 2024, the BoE, PRA, and FCA jointly published the final rules for the CTP oversight regime via Policy Statement PS16/24 (PRA) and PS24/16 (FCA). Key provisions:

- Rules are effective from 1 January 2025 but apply only once HM Treasury issues a designation order naming specific entities as CTPs
- HM Treasury has not yet designated any CTPs as of April 2026 — the regime is live in structure but dormant in application
- Designated CTPs will be subject to new resilience obligations (SS6/24 is the primary guidance for CTPs themselves)
- The regime is technology-neutral — not limited to cloud providers
- Designation criteria: HM Treasury may designate where failure or disruption of the third party's services "could threaten the stability of, or confidence in, the UK financial system"

Sources: [FCA PS24/16](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps24-16-operational-resilience-critical-third-parties-uk-financial-sector); [Hogan Lovells — UK CTP Regime Rules Published](https://www.hoganlovells.com/en/publications/uk-critical-third-parties-oversight-regime-rules-published); [Global Financial Reg Blog, November 2024](https://www.globalfinregblog.com/2024/11/uk-regulators-publish-final-rules-for-critical-third-parties/)

**Implications for Guidewire Cloud and major cloud providers.** No cloud provider, including AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud, and no insurance SaaS platform, including Guidewire, has been designated as a CTP as of April 2026. However:
- The PRA Business Plan 2026/27 explicitly references continuing the CTP regime and advancing initial designations with HM Treasury
- Guidewire is unlikely to be a systemically significant CTP candidate given its market concentration in insurance only, but the major hyperscale cloud providers (AWS, Azure, GCP) are primary candidates for future designation
- Atlas should nonetheless treat Guidewire Cloud as a material third-party arrangement requiring full SS2/21 / PS7/26 governance, regardless of CTP designation status

Sources: [BoE/PRA/FCA Approach to CTP Oversight (2024)](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/-/media/boe/files/prudential-regulation/approach/critical-third-parties-approach-2024.pdf); [Addleshaw Goddard, January 2025](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2025/financial-regulation/financial-regulation-in-the-know-uk-eu-banking-investment-regulation-january-2025/boe-pra-fca-policy-statement-critical-third-parties-oversight-regime/)

**PRA Business Plan 2026/27 commitment.** The PRA will "continue to work with the FCA to advance the new regime for Critical Third Parties to mitigate risks and vulnerabilities arising from systemic third-party dependencies." This signals active progress toward first designations, likely in 2026 or 2027.

Source: [Regulation Tomorrow — PRA Business Plan 2026/27](https://www.regulationtomorrow.com/2026/04/prudential-regulation-authority-business-plan-2026-27/)

### What is uncertain

- The timeline for first HM Treasury CTP designations is not published
- Whether AWS (Atlas's CTO preference) or Azure (Atlas's CISO preference) will be among the first designated CTPs is unknown, though both are likely candidates given their systemic concentration in UK financial services
- Designation of a CTP does not directly change obligations on firms using them, but it would intensify regulatory scrutiny of those dependencies and could require firms to evidence deeper resilience testing with that provider

## Q5: PRA Findings and Thematic Reviews on Insurer Technology Transformation Risk

### What is established

**No standalone published thematic review on insurer technology transformation has been identified** in publicly available PRA publications as of April 2026. Supervisory findings on technology risk have been communicated through:

- Annual Dear CEO letters (2024, 2025, 2026)
- The PRA Business Plan 2024/25, 2025/26, and 2026/27
- Bilateral supervisory engagement (not public)

**Published thematic expectations (synthesised from letters and business plans):**

1. **Legacy technology as a named risk.** The PRA has explicitly called out legacy technology obsolescence as a key vulnerability across multiple letters. Firms are expected to have credible remediation plans. The 2026 letter adds that "transformation and cloud usage must be tightly managed to avoid new risks."

2. **Transformation governance.** Boards must factor resilience into major change initiatives. The phrase "boards and SMFs should monitor and manage the risks arising from transformation journeys" is explicit in the 2025 letter and repeated in 2026 framing. This is a direct signal that core system replacement programmes are within supervisory scope.

3. **Data quality.** The PRA's 2026 priorities flag "ongoing weaknesses in exposure monitoring" and the need for "investment in fit-for-purpose systems across parts of the market." For Atlas, whose data reconciliation failure triggered enhanced supervision, this is directly relevant. Regulatory reporting data integrity is a named priority.

4. **AI adoption risk.** The PRA frames AI as "both an enabler and a risk" and expects firms to "adopt new technologies without compromising on safety and soundness." AI amplifies data quality issues and third-party dependencies — a compounding risk in a transformation context.

5. **2024 Cyber Stress Test findings.** The PRA conducted a cyber stress test in 2024 and indicated findings would be published in 2025 H2. These findings would be relevant to Atlas's target-state cyber architecture design. Availability of published findings was not confirmed in sources accessible during this research.

Sources: [LCP — PRA 2026 Priorities for General Insurers](https://www.lcp.com/en/insights/in-brief/pra-regulatory-priorities-for-general-insurers-2026-what-to-focus-on); [Clifford Chance Insurance Insights, 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/insurance-insights/2026/01/the-pras-2026-insurance-supervision-priorities.html); [Pro Global — PRA 2025 Supervisory Priorities](https://pro-global.com/navigating-the-pras-2025-supervisory-priorities-a-roadmap-for-insurers/)

### What is uncertain

- The PRA has not published a dedicated technology transformation thematic review for the insurance sector (equivalent to, for example, the Bank's published CBEST thematic reviews for banking). Whether one is in preparation is unknown.
- The 2024 cyber stress test findings report (expected H2 2025) has not been confirmed as published in available sources. This should be checked directly on the Bank of England website.

## Open Gaps for Atlas Architecture Team Follow-Up

1. **PS7/26 classification.** Confirm whether Atlas is classified as "standard" or "enhanced" under PS7/26 reporting tiers given its enhanced supervisory status. Determine who at the PRA to engage.

2. **Pre-signature MTP notification for Guidewire.** If Atlas is proceeding to contract with Guidewire, PS7/26 notification must occur before "internal or external commitments are finalised" (from March 2027 effective date — but given the transformation timeline, early engagement is prudent, and the PRA supervisory expectation under SS2/21 applies now).

3. **ICT/cyber consultation (H2 2025) — check status.** Confirm whether the PRA/FCA joint consultation on ICT and cyber risk policy launched, closed, and resulted in a policy statement. If finalised, it may introduce new ICT risk standards with direct bearing on cloud architecture design choices.

4. **2024 Cyber Stress Test findings report.** Expected H2 2025. Check BoE website for publication — findings will be directly relevant to Atlas's target-state security architecture design.

5. **Guidewire Cloud exit plan and escrow.** SS2/21 requires documented and tested exit plans for material third-party arrangements. For a core policy admin SaaS platform, this must include data portability, transition timeline, and interim arrangements. This is an architecture design requirement, not just a procurement clause.

6. **CTP designation watch.** If AWS is chosen as the underlying cloud provider (CTO preference), monitor HM Treasury CTP designation activity. A CTP designation for AWS would not change Atlas's direct obligations but would intensify supervisory scrutiny of AWS dependencies and require deeper resilience testing evidence.

7. **CBEST / STAR-FS applicability.** Confirm with the PRA supervisory team whether Atlas's enhanced supervision status triggers STAR-FS participation and whether the target-state cloud architecture needs to be scoped into the test.

8. **DyGIST (May 2026).** Confirm Atlas's participation scope. The dynamic GI stress test runs during Phase 1 of the transformation programme. Ensure the programme team is aware and that any interim dual-run system states are considered in the stress test scenario design.
